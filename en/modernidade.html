Death in Modernity



The subject of death is by no means a contemporary discussion. Throughout history numerous philosophers, historians, sociologists, biologists, anthropologists and psychologists have explored this question. This is because death does not fit into a specific category; it transcends history and is, above all, an inherently human issue. Just like birth, death is an intrinsic element of the human life cycle. Therefore, from a biological point of view, it is a completely natural phenomenon. However, human beings are different, human being is distinguished, above all, by their ability to attribute symbolic meanings and values to experiences. experiences. As a result, the meaning of death is highly variable throughout history and between different human cultures. between different human cultures.

The beginning of today's vision

From the Middle Ages onwards, the European vision we have today began to emerge. series of negative connotations began to be attached to death: dark, macabre ideas, as well as conceptions of torture and suffering came to be associated with death, generating a deep discomfort in people this disturbing event. Research done

The research we conducted aimed to provide a clearer picture of opinions, fears, hopes and philosophies that people have regarding death. Through a questionnaire, we sought to capture the differences in people's views on death, from the religious and spiritual dimensions and legacies personal.
In this study, we will share the results that emerged from public responses, contributing to a deeper understanding of diverse perspectives and attitudes towards death. After all, this research not only offers clearer insight into how people approach the end of life, but also highlights the importance of openly discussing this universal topic, which invariably touches us all.

The audience of 38 people was asked the following questions:
“What do you think about death?”
31.6% responded that it was the end of everything, 18.4% responded that it was not thought about, 15.8 responded that it was what the interviewee's religion dictated, 15.8% that it was what the said philosophy, 13.2% preferred not to talk about it;
“Are you afraid of death?”
47.4% answered yes, 31.6% answered no and another 21.1% maybe;
“Would you like to know more about the subject?”
81.6% answered yes while 18.4% answered no.
