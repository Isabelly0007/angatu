Ancient Greece

In Ancient Greece, death was seen as a natural part of life, and there was no unified conception of what happened after death. Different conversations and myths about the fate of the soul after death varied from one region to another and over time. The ancient Greeks performed elaborate rituals to honor the dead. This included washing and preparing the body, often with the help of professionals called "prosthesis" and "nekromanteia" (divination of the dead). After the body was prepared, there was a funeral procession to the burial site. The most common burial method in Ancient Greece was inhumation, i.e. burying the body in a grave. The Greeks highly valued the idea of a proper burial to ensure that the soul of the deceased had a safe passage to the afterlife. Ancient Egypt

The Egyptians firmly believed in life after death. They believed that death was not the end, but the beginning of a journey to the next world, where the soul (ka and ba) of the deceased would continue to exist. One of the best-known aspects of Egyptian funerary culture is the practice of mummification. They believed that the preservation of the body was essential for the survival of the soul in the afterlife. The mummification process was complex and involved

removing the internal organs, drying the body and protecting it with bandages. The Book of the Dead, a collection of texts and spells, was often placed with the deceased to help them on their journey to the afterlife and provide instructions on how to overcome obstacles and reach the afterlife. They built elaborate tombs, like pyramids, to house the bodies of pharaohs and people of high social standing. The tombs were filled with treasures, food, and other goods that the deceased might need in the afterlife, and they believed in various gods associated with death and the afterlife, such as Osiris and Anubis. The Egyptians made regular offerings to the spirits of the dead, including food, drink and personal belongings, to ensure that they were treated well in the afterlife. Every year, there was the Orion Festival, where the Egyptians believed that the souls of the dead temporarily returned to the world of the living. This was an opportunity to pay homage to the ancestors.
